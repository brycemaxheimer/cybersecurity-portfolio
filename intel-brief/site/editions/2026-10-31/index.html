What happened

On 2026-10-30 CISA added two Ivanti Connect Secure vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, citing evidence of active exploitation. According to CISA, CVE-2026-118527 is a predictable-identifier flaw that lets a remote authenticated attacker upload files with attacker-controlled content to a known location on disk. CVE-2026-111357 is a missing-authentication flaw that lets a local authenticated attacker escalate privileges. CISA says both can be chained with each other and with a third flaw, CVE-2026-104935; the items give no details on that CVE, affected versions, or fixed releases.

Why it matters

Ivanti Connect Secure is a widely deployed internet-facing VPN gateway, and CISA's description points to a chain that moves from file upload to privilege escalation, which could give an attacker full control of the appliance and a foothold into internal networks. Exploitation is confirmed. The KEV due dates are 2026-11-02 for CVE-2026-111357 and 2026-11-13 for CVE-2026-118527. The low EPSS of 0.00316 for CVE-2026-111357 should not lower priority given the confirmed in-the-wild use.

What to do

  1. Apply Ivanti's mitigations or patches for CVE-2026-111357, CVE-2026-118527 and the chained CVE-2026-104935 on every Connect Secure appliance, prioritizing internet-facing units, before the KEV due dates of 2026-11-02 and 2026-11-13.
  2. Before and after patching, perform a forensic triage of each appliance as CISA's KEV entries require, including running Ivanti's integrity checking tool if available and reviewing for unexpected files on disk and new admin accounts.
  3. If compromise is suspected, rotate credentials that transit or are stored on the appliance: local admin accounts, LDAP/AD service accounts, and VPN user credentials. Also revoke related sessions in Entra ID.
  4. Hunt internal telemetry for logons, scanning, or lateral movement originating from the appliance's internal IP addresses since late October 2026.
  5. If mitigations cannot be applied, restrict or take the appliance offline, as CISA advises discontinuing use when mitigations are unavailable.

Microsoft: Teams help desk impersonation leads to AD CS ESC1 and ESC8 abuse in two intrusions

Active Directory Certificate Services · 1 source · first reported 30 October 2026

What happened

Microsoft Threat Intelligence reports two human-operated intrusions. In both, attackers impersonated IT or help desk staff over Microsoft Teams messages and calls and talked users into granting remote access, mostly through Quick Assist. After connecting, they staged portable Python, Bun or Java runtimes, ran host and network discovery, and assessed Active Directory Certificate Services. In one case the attacker abused an ESC1-vulnerable certificate template to get certificates for privileged accounts and performed DCSync. In the other, the attacker used PetitPotam to relay a domain controller's NTLM authentication to AD CS Web Enrollment (ESC8), then used the resulting domain-controller certificate for PKINIT Kerberos authentication.

Why it matters

Microsoft says no Teams vulnerability was involved. The attacks relied on social engineering plus insecure or overly permissive AD CS configurations, which are common in Windows domains, and exploitation was confirmed in real environments. In Case 1, a privileged identity excluded from Defender automated containment stayed usable and later supported data exfiltration, so exclusions and AD CS misconfigurations deserve immediate review.

What to do

  1. Audit AD CS certificate templates for ESC1 conditions (enrollee-supplied subject, client authentication EKU, broad enrollment rights) and remove or restrict them.
  2. Disable or harden AD CS Web Enrollment against NTLM relay (ESC8) by requiring HTTPS with Extended Protection for Authentication or removing the role, and mitigate PetitPotam-style coerced authentication on domain controllers.
  3. Review identities excluded from Defender automatic attack disruption, especially privileged accounts, and remove unnecessary exclusions.
  4. Restrict Teams external access and chats from unknown tenants, and limit or block Quick Assist where an approved remote support tool exists.
  5. Train users and help desk staff to verify IT contact through a known channel before granting remote access.

What happened

Rapid7 describes an origin-validation flaw in BeyondTrust Remote Support. An unauthenticated attacker using the public customer session entry point can forge a local router address and reach an internal file-transfer service. This lets them write arbitrary files, including a PHP file in the web root, and run code as the site's OS user. Rapid7 confirmed the chain on Remote Support 24.1.2 (build 1524046) with default thin chat and public session starts enabled, and says a Metasploit module now implements it. According to the enrichment data, the CVE carries CVSS 9.3 and an EPSS of about 0.19, and it is listed in CISA KEV, although the item gives no KEV due date.

Why it matters

Remote Support appliances are internet-facing by design and hold privileged access to the endpoints and servers that technicians support. A compromised appliance can therefore become a launch point into Windows estates. With KEV listing, the story labelled as exploited in the wild, and a public Metasploit module, opportunistic mass exploitation is likely. Rapid7 has not established the full affected range or fixed builds, so every exposed instance should be treated as at risk until BeyondTrust says otherwise.

What to do

  1. Inventory all BeyondTrust Remote Support appliances (cloud and on-premises) and apply BeyondTrust's fix for CVE-2026-102764 as soon as the vendor publishes it; confirm the fixed build directly with BeyondTrust because no source states one.
  2. Until patched, restrict internet access to the appliance's public customer portal where business allows, and consider disabling public issue-menu session starts and thin chat, which were enabled on Rapid7's vulnerable test appliance.
  3. Review appliance file systems and web logs for unexpected PHP files in the public web directory and for requests to newly created scripts; involve BeyondTrust support for a forensic review if anything is found.
  4. Hunt on Windows endpoints for unusual commands, shells or file drops launched by the BeyondTrust customer or jump client, which would indicate an attacker using the appliance to reach endpoints.
  5. If compromise is suspected, rotate credentials stored in or used through Remote Support, including vault credentials and representative accounts, and review session logs for unknown sessions.

SecurityWeek: PHP2Shell attacks reportedly root Palo Alto firewalls, fail against WordPress

Palo Alto Networks · 1 source · first reported 30 October 2026

What happened

SecurityWeek reports that attacks dubbed PHP2Shell, which target critical PHP flaws, have been ongoing for more than a week and have reportedly gained root access on Palo Alto Networks firewalls, while attempts against WordPress sites failed. The outlet says more than 22.6 million exposed instances may be affected. The only available item is a short summary, so it does not name CVE identifiers, affected PHP or PAN-OS versions, patches, or indicators.

Why it matters

Root compromise of a perimeter firewall gives an attacker a foothold that bypasses most endpoint telemetry, and exploitation is reported as active. Any internet-facing Palo Alto device or Windows or Linux server running PHP may be exposed. Details are thin, so treat this as an exposure review until Palo Alto Networks or PHP advisories confirm scope and fixes.

What to do

  1. Check Palo Alto Networks security advisories for any PAN-OS issue linked to these PHP flaws, and apply fixes or mitigations as soon as they are published.
  2. Restrict management interfaces on Palo Alto firewalls to trusted internal networks. Review device logs and configuration for unexpected admin accounts, changes, or outbound connections.
  3. Inventory internet-facing hosts running PHP and update PHP to the latest supported release once the specific flaws are identified.
  4. Hunt on Windows web servers for PHP processes launching shells or scripting engines, and for new script files written to web roots.
  5. Track follow-up reporting for CVE IDs and indicators, then re-run the hunts with those details.

What happened

CISA says attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-97229, a maximum-severity GitLab vulnerability that was patched earlier this month, according to BleepingComputer. SecurityWeek reports that the flaw lets unauthenticated attackers run arbitrary code on the GitLab server, and its headline says exploitation hit Alpine Linux, though the items give no further detail. The CVE is in CISA KEV, and enrichment shows public proof-of-concept code. Neither source names affected or fixed versions.

Why it matters

Self-managed GitLab servers hold source code, CI/CD secrets, deploy keys and runner tokens. A server compromise can spread into build pipelines, cloud accounts and the wider network, and internet-facing instances are the most exposed. With CVSS 10.0, confirmed exploitation, KEV listing, public PoC and an EPSS of about 0.27, unpatched instances should be treated as an emergency. KEV lists no due date.

What to do

  1. Find every self-managed GitLab instance, internet-facing ones first, and upgrade to the patched release in GitLab's security advisory for CVE-2026-97229; the sources do not name version numbers.
  2. If you cannot patch immediately, remove public exposure by putting the instance behind VPN or an IP allowlist until it is upgraded.
  3. Hunt on GitLab hosts for shells, downloaders or interpreters started by GitLab Puma, Ruby or Workhorse processes, and for unexpected outbound connections from those processes.
  4. For any instance that was exposed and unpatched, review GitLab application and web logs for anomalous unauthenticated requests. Check for new admin users, personal access tokens, SSH keys and runners.
  5. If you suspect compromise, rotate CI/CD variables, runner registration tokens, deploy keys and cloud credentials stored in GitLab.
