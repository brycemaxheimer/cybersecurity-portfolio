What happened

SonicWall has patched CVE-2026-19868 in SonicOS, and SecurityWeek reports that the flaw is being exploited. According to SecurityWeek, an attacker who already holds valid credentials for a different login method can get past SAML checks, including multi-factor authentication enforced through SAML. CISA lists the CVE in its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, and public proof-of-concept code exists. The sources do not name affected or fixed SonicOS versions.

Why it matters

Organizations that rely on SAML-enforced MFA for SonicWall VPN access may be exposed: stolen or reused local or LDAP credentials could be enough to reach the internal network without passing the MFA step. The flaw is rated CVSS 7.5 with an EPSS of 0.6%, but confirmed exploitation, KEV listing and public PoC code make it urgent regardless of the low score. CISA has not published a due date.

What to do

  1. Apply SonicWall's SonicOS update for CVE-2026-19868 on all firewalls and VPN gateways, using the version listed in SonicWall's advisory.
  2. Until patched, disable or restrict non-SAML authentication methods (local and LDAP users) on VPN portals where SAML with MFA is meant to be enforced.
  3. Review SonicWall VPN authentication logs for successful logins that used a non-SAML method by users who are supposed to authenticate only through SAML, especially from unfamiliar IP addresses.
  4. Reset credentials for any VPN accounts with suspicious logins, and rotate local SonicWall account passwords.
  5. Hunt for internal lateral movement originating from VPN-assigned address pools after any suspicious VPN session.

Crédit Mutuel Arkéa hit by attack via service provider enabling up to $133 million in fraudulent transactions

1 source · first reported 7 November 2026

What happened

BleepingComputer reports that French cooperative bank Crédit Mutuel Arkéa was targeted in a cyberattack, reportedly carried out through a service provider, that enabled fraudulent transactions of up to $133 million. According to the report, the bank responded by suspending certain systems. The sources do not describe the initial access method, the provider involved, the systems affected, or the attackers.

Why it matters

The incident shows that compromise of a trusted third party can be enough to move large sums through a bank's systems, which makes supplier access paths a priority for financial-sector defenders. Because no technical details, indicators, or vulnerabilities have been published, there is nothing specific to hunt for yet. The practical value now is to review how much access your own service providers hold.

What to do

  1. Inventory service provider and MSP accounts, remote access tools and VPN connections that can reach payment or core banking systems, and remove any that are unused.
  2. Require phishing-resistant MFA and conditional access for all third-party accounts in Entra ID, and limit them to named locations or devices where possible.
  3. Review recent sign-ins and privileged actions by vendor and guest accounts for unusual times, locations or volumes.
  4. Confirm that high-value or bulk transactions require out-of-band approval that a single compromised provider account cannot bypass.
  5. Watch for follow-up reporting from the bank or French authorities that names the provider, the access method or indicators.

What happened

NVD published CVE-2026-123966 on 7 November 2026. It is a heap-based buffer overflow in the saveParentControlInfo handler of Tenda AC19 firmware 16.03.10.07, triggered through the deviceId parameter. NVD rates it CVSS 9.3 and says it can be reached remotely and that an exploit has been publicly disclosed. The sources do not mention a vendor advisory or fixed firmware, and the enrichment data does not confirm that working proof-of-concept code exists, so the public-exploit claim rests on NVD alone.

Why it matters

The AC19 is a consumer and small-office router, so it is most likely to appear at branch sites, home offices of remote workers, or as unmanaged shadow IT, not in core enterprise edge estates. The flaw is not in CISA KEV and has an EPSS of 1.0%, but public exploit details and remote reachability mean any exposed management interface is at real risk of compromise and of being turned into a botnet node or pivot point.

What to do

  1. Inventory Tenda AC19 routers on corporate and branch networks, and treat any found as untrusted until a fixed firmware is confirmed.
  2. Make sure the router web management interface is not reachable from the internet or from guest and untrusted network segments.
  3. Replace AC19 devices in business use with managed, supported edge equipment if Tenda does not publish a fix.
  4. Check Tenda's support channels for a firmware update that addresses CVE-2026-123966, and apply it once available.
  5. Watch for unexpected outbound traffic from router IP addresses that could show compromise or botnet enrollment.

Explainer

Background, not news

Trusted relationship attacks: breaching a target through its service providers

A trusted relationship attack compromises a third party that already has legitimate access to the real target, such as an IT provider, payments processor or software vendor, and then uses that access to reach the target's systems.

Why today. Crédit Mutuel Arkéa's losses of up to $133 million reportedly came through a service provider, not a direct breach of the bank's own perimeter. That pattern deserves its own threat model, separate from patching edge devices. See story 2.

How it works

Organizations give outside parties standing access: managed service providers run remote management tools, outsourcers hold VPN accounts, Microsoft partners hold delegated admin rights in customer tenants, and integrators connect to payment or core banking systems through APIs and service accounts. MITRE ATT&CK tracks this as Trusted Relationship (T1199) under Initial Access.

The attacker targets the provider, which is often smaller and less monitored than its customers. Once inside, they use the provider's existing credentials, tools and network paths. To the victim, the activity arrives from an expected source, through an approved channel, with valid authentication.

What the attacker gains

The main gain is trust that has already been granted. Provider connections are often allow-listed at the firewall, excluded from Conditional Access or MFA prompts, and run with broad privileges because nobody scoped them tightly. Activity from a provider's remote management agent or admin account looks like normal maintenance.

A compromised provider can also lead to many customers at once. In a financial setting, the attacker may go straight for business processes, such as initiating or approving transactions through the provider's integration, rather than deploying malware that endpoint tools would catch. The public reporting on the Arkéa case does not say which route was used.

What it looks like in telemetry

In Entra ID, sign-ins by a Microsoft partner through delegated admin access are recorded in the customer tenant with a cross-tenant access type indicating a service provider, and the actor's home tenant differs from the resource tenant. B2B guest sign-ins show up as cross-tenant too. Unusual source IPs, times or operations on these sign-ins are worth hunting.

On endpoints, look for remote management and remote access tools spawning shells, scripting engines or credential access tools, or starting sessions outside the provider's normal maintenance windows. In Defender XDR, the parent process of suspicious activity often turns out to be the provider's agent. Baselining what that agent normally does makes deviations stand out.

How to reduce the risk

Keep an inventory of every third party with access: accounts, tenants, agents, VPN profiles, API keys and network paths. Then cut each one down to least privilege and limit it in time. For Microsoft partners, replace legacy delegated admin privileges with granular delegated admin privileges (GDAP), which assign specific Entra roles for a defined period instead of standing Global Administrator.

Don't exempt provider access from the controls you apply to staff. Require phishing-resistant MFA, enforce Conditional Access on cross-tenant and guest access, and use just-in-time activation where possible. For high-value business actions such as payments, add an out-of-band approval step that a single compromised integration cannot complete by itself.

For defenders

  1. List every tenant with delegated admin or GDAP access to your Entra ID tenant, and remove legacy delegated admin relationships and roles you don't need.
  2. In Sentinel, hunt SigninLogs for cross-tenant sign-ins by service providers and guests. Alert on new source IPs, off-hours activity and privileged operations by those identities.
  3. Inventory remote management and remote access agents installed by providers, and build Defender XDR detections for those agents launching shells, scripting engines or credential dumping tools.
  4. Review Conditional Access policies for exclusions covering vendor accounts, guest users or partner IP ranges, and remove exclusions that are not strictly needed.
  5. For payment and other high-impact workflows, confirm that actions coming from third-party integrations need independent approval, and that their API credentials are scoped and rotated.
