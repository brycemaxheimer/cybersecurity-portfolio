What happened

CISA added CVE-2026-103406 to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 25 November 2026, indicating confirmed exploitation in the wild. According to CISA, HPE Juniper Session Smart Conductor and Session Smart Router contain an OS command injection flaw. A remote attacker can use a specially crafted Salt minion ID to run arbitrary commands as root. The sources do not list affected or fixed versions, and CISA directs organizations to apply mitigations according to vendor instructions.

Why it matters

Session Smart Conductor manages fleets of SD-WAN routers, so root code execution there could give an attacker control over routing edge devices across many sites. The flaw is rated CVSS 9.8, public proof-of-concept code reportedly exists, and the KEV due date is 28 November 2026, only three days after it was listed. EPSS is low at 1.5%, but confirmed exploitation outweighs that score.

What to do

  1. Inventory all Juniper Session Smart Conductor and Session Smart Router deployments and apply the HPE Juniper fix or mitigation for CVE-2026-103406 before 28 November 2026.
  2. Restrict the Conductor's Salt management ports (TCP 4505 and 4506) and its management interfaces so only known router and administrator addresses can reach them, and never expose them to the internet.
  3. Review the Conductor's list of accepted Salt minions and remove any unknown or oddly named minion IDs.
  4. Follow CISA's forensics triage guidance on exposed Conductors: collect logs and look for unexpected root processes, new accounts, cron entries or SSH keys.
  5. If you find signs of compromise, rotate the credentials and keys stored on the Conductor and managed routers, and rebuild from known-good images.

Palo Alto Networks warns attackers are chaining PHP2Shell and Fragnesia flaws against PAN-OS

Palo Alto Networks PAN-OS · 1 source · first reported 25 November 2026

What happened

According to BleepingComputer, Palo Alto Networks has warned that threat actors are chaining exploits for two vulnerabilities, named PHP2Shell and Fragnesia, to attack PAN-OS devices. The activity targets devices whose management interfaces are exposed to the internet. The available reporting gives no CVE identifiers, affected or fixed versions, or technical indicators, so check Palo Alto Networks' own advisory for those details.

Why it matters

PAN-OS firewalls and GlobalProtect gateways sit on the network perimeter, and a compromised management plane gives an attacker a foothold to pivot into internal Windows and identity infrastructure. Exploitation is reported as active. Any PAN-OS device with its management interface reachable from the internet should be treated as at risk until the vendor's fixes are confirmed applied.

What to do

  1. Immediately remove internet access to PAN-OS management interfaces and restrict them to a dedicated management network or allowlisted administrator IPs.
  2. Review Palo Alto Networks' security advisory for PHP2Shell and Fragnesia, identify the affected PAN-OS versions, and apply the vendor's fixed releases or mitigations as soon as possible.
  3. Review PAN-OS system, configuration and management-plane logs for unexpected administrator logins, configuration changes, new accounts or unfamiliar files since at least early November 2026.
  4. If compromise is suspected, rotate firewall administrator credentials, API keys and any secrets stored on the device, such as LDAP or RADIUS bind accounts, and follow the vendor's incident guidance.
  5. Watch internal hosts for inbound connections that originate from firewall interface addresses, which may indicate an attacker pivoting from a compromised device.

Drupal security team confirms in-the-wild exploitation of critical RCE flaw in unpatched sites

Drupal Drupal · 1 source · first reported 25 November 2026

What happened

According to BleepingComputer, the Drupal security team has confirmed that attackers are exploiting a critical vulnerability that allows them to take over unpatched Drupal sites. Exploitation has reportedly been under way since at least the Thursday before the 25 November 2026 report. The available reporting does not name a CVE, the affected Drupal core or module versions, or the fixed releases.

Why it matters

Internet-facing Drupal sites, including marketing sites, portals and intranets on Windows or Linux hosts, can be fully taken over, giving attackers a foothold for web shells, credential theft and lateral movement. Exploitation is confirmed and described as widespread, so any unpatched instance should be treated as possibly compromised.

What to do

  1. Inventory every Drupal instance you run, including forgotten or third-party hosted sites, and apply the fix from the Drupal security team's advisory immediately.
  2. If you cannot patch right away, take exposed Drupal sites offline or restrict them behind a WAF or IP allowlist until they are updated.
  3. Treat sites left unpatched since mid-November 2026 as potentially compromised: check for web server processes spawning shells and for new or modified PHP files in the web root.
  4. Rotate the database credentials, the Drupal admin accounts and any secrets stored in the configuration of any site that shows signs of compromise.
  5. Check the Drupal security advisories for the CVE, affected versions and any vendor indicators, since current reporting gives few details.

What happened

On 24 November 2026 Broadcom published VMSA-2026-0011, which fixes CVE-2026-123903 (CVSS 9.8), an authentication bypass in the VMware Directory Service (vmdir) of vCenter that lets a network attacker reach vCenter without credentials over LDAP. The same advisory fixes several high and medium severity flaws (CVE-2026-123904 through CVE-2026-123909). Rapid7 says a public proof-of-concept exists and that it confirmed the bypass works, but it knows of no in-the-wild exploitation of this CVE as of 25 November 2026. Broadcom describes it as separate from CVE-2026-123890, an RPC-reachable vmdir bypass that has been exploited in the wild and is listed in CISA KEV.

Why it matters

vCenter controls the whole virtualization estate, including Windows servers and domain controllers running as VMs, so unauthenticated access is a direct path to mass impact such as ransomware deployment. Attackers are already targeting vmdir through CVE-2026-123890, and a working public PoC now exists for the LDAP variant (EPSS 0.6%, not in KEV). Broadcom lists no workaround and the updates cannot be applied through Quick Patch.

What to do

  1. Update vCenter and related VCF/vSphere Foundation deployments to the fixed versions outside the normal patch cycle; vCenter 8.0 should be on 8.0 U3m.
  2. Confirm CVE-2026-123890 is also remediated, since it is exploited in the wild and in CISA KEV.
  3. Restrict network access to vCenter management and directory service (LDAP and RPC) interfaces to dedicated admin networks and jump hosts only.
  4. Review vCenter and SSO logs for logins, new SSO users or group changes, and VM power or snapshot operations that do not match change records.
  5. For vCenter 7.0 extended-support customers, open a Broadcom Support case now and apply strict network isolation until patched.

What happened

CISA added CVE-2026-133469, a flaw in Vates Xen Orchestra, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 25 November 2026. According to CISA, Xen Orchestra does not properly validate the type of input in authentication tokens. An unauthenticated attacker can send a crafted token holding an empty JSON object to skip authentication and act with administrative privileges. The sources do not list affected or fixed versions, and no vendor advisory was available to confirm them.

Why it matters

Xen Orchestra is the management plane for XCP-ng and XenServer hypervisors, so an attacker with admin access can control, snapshot, export or destroy every virtual machine it manages, including domain controllers and other Windows servers. Exploitation is confirmed in the wild and public proof-of-concept code exists. The CVSS score is 9.8, EPSS is 31.8%, and the KEV due date is 28 November 2026, which leaves very little time to act.

What to do

  1. Take every Xen Orchestra web interface off the internet now, and allow access only from a management VLAN or VPN.
  2. Apply the update or mitigation in the Vates advisory by 28 November 2026. Check the advisory for the fixed version, because the sources do not state one.
  3. Review Xen Orchestra audit logs and user lists for new admin accounts, new API tokens, VM exports, snapshots, console sessions or deletions you cannot explain, especially from external IP addresses.
  4. If you find signs of compromise, rotate Xen Orchestra credentials, hypervisor pool credentials and any secrets stored in managed VMs, and follow CISA forensic triage guidance before rebuilding.
  5. Check backup and replication jobs that Xen Orchestra controls for tampering, and confirm that offline backups are intact.

Explainer

Background, not news

The CISA KEV catalog: what an entry means and what it does not

The Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is CISA's list of CVEs with reliable evidence of exploitation in the wild, and it sets remediation deadlines that bind US federal civilian agencies and serve as a prioritization signal for everyone else.

Why today. CISA added 10 flaws to KEV on 25 November 2026, covering Juniper Session Smart routers, Xen Orchestra, Danfoss refrigeration controllers and Mitel MiCollab. Meanwhile, the exploited Drupal and PAN-OS attacks show what KEV does not yet cover. See story 7, story 1, story 5, story 6, story 9, story 4, story 2, story 3.

What gets a CVE into KEV

CISA adds a vulnerability to KEV when three conditions hold: it has an assigned CVE ID, there is reliable evidence that it has been actively exploited, and there is clear remediation guidance such as a patch, a mitigation or a workaround. A public proof-of-concept alone does not qualify. That is why the newly fixed vCenter bypass CVE-2026-123903, which has a validated public exploit but no confirmed attacks, is not listed, while the separate vmdir flaw CVE-2026-123890 is.

Each entry records the vendor, product, a short description, the date added, a due date, the required action, and a flag showing whether the flaw is known to be used in ransomware campaigns. The required action often reads as applying mitigations per vendor instructions, or discontinuing the product if no mitigation is available. That wording appears even when no fixed version has been published, as with several of today's additions.

Deadlines and who they bind

Binding Operational Directive 22-01 requires US federal civilian executive branch agencies to remediate KEV entries by their due date. CISA sets the due date per entry, and for new additions it is typically a few weeks after the date added. Private companies and non-US organizations have no legal obligation, but many treat KEV due dates as an outside limit for internet-facing assets.

Age does not decide the deadline. A flaw from 2023, like the two Danfoss CVEs added today, gets a fresh due date when it enters the catalog. Its age tells you nothing about how urgent it is now.

What KEV does not tell you

KEV is a confirmation list, so it lags behind attacks. A vendor or security team can confirm exploitation days before CISA adds the CVE, and some exploited flaws never get a CVE quickly enough to be listed. Today's Drupal and PAN-OS reports are examples: both are being exploited, and neither has a public CVE in the reporting yet. Not being in KEV does not mean a flaw is safe to defer.

Entries also carry almost no threat detail. You will not find indicators, attacker names, targeting, or usually affected version ranges, so you need the vendor advisory to scope exposure and threat intelligence to hunt. EPSS answers a different question: it estimates the probability of exploitation activity in the coming weeks. It complements KEV but does not replace it.

For defenders

  1. Check your exposure management data for the 10 CVEs added on 25 November 2026, especially internet-reachable Juniper Session Smart routers and conductors, Mitel MiCollab servers and Xen Orchestra instances. Treat any you find as compromise candidates as well as patch targets.
  2. Pull the KEV catalog's machine-readable feed into Sentinel as a watchlist and join it against Defender Vulnerability Management software vulnerability data. That gives you a standing list of exposed KEV CVEs with their due dates.
  3. Set an internal remediation SLA for KEV entries on edge and management-plane devices that is no longer than CISA's federal due date. Track separately any entry whose vendor guidance is mitigation-only.
  4. Do not wait for KEV when a vendor confirms exploitation. Act on the Drupal and PAN-OS reports now: restrict PAN-OS management interfaces to trusted networks and patch Drupal sites as soon as fixed releases are confirmed.
  5. For KEV entries flagged as used in ransomware campaigns, add hunting for post-exploitation activity on the affected hosts. Patching alone does not remove an attacker who is already in.
